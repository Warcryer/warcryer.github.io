Warcryer vs Discord

Discord is where most alliances already live, and nothing here asks you to leave it. Its voice channels are made for a room of people talking. A siege with a chain of command is a different shape: hundreds of players, several layers of leaders, and orders that must be heard over party chatter.

Checked against Discord's own pages on 18 November 2026. Warcryer is in beta.

The short answer

Choose Discord if

  • Your events fit in one voice channel, or a few that do not need to hear each other.
  • Your players are on phones, macOS or Linux.
  • You want one tool for everything, and the event is a small part of what your community does there.
  • You already run sign-up bots that your players know.

Choose Warcryer if

  • You field more people than one voice channel holds, in parties that each need their own talk.
  • Leaders need a channel of their own while still hearing their party.
  • An order from the event leader has to reach everyone, in every party, at once.
  • You want attendance from who was actually in voice, not from who clicked a reaction.

What is different in practice

Several nets at once, not one channel

In Discord you are in one voice channel. A party leader who must hear the alliance's command channel has to leave their party's channel to do it, or run a second account. In Warcryer every player is on their party, their clan and Broadcast at the same moment, leaders on Command as well, each with its own key.

Past 99 without turning the alliance into an audience

A Discord voice channel holds up to 99 people. Beyond that the answer is many channels, which then cannot hear one call, or a Stage channel, where most people are listeners. Warcryer's Broadcast reaches everyone in the operation while each party keeps talking among itself.

The roster places people

On Discord someone has to get three hundred players into the right thirty channels and keep them there. In Warcryer the parties are built on a board beforehand, from who signed up, and starting the operation puts every player on their nets.

Works with your Discord, not against it

Players sign in to Warcryer with their Discord account. Connect a channel and every new, changed or started operation is announced there, with a reminder before the start and a role to ping if you choose. Keep Discord for the community, and use Warcryer when the event starts.

Where Discord is the better choice

Warcryer does one thing, and it is young. These are its limits today.

  • It is a Windows app. There is a browser version for a quick look, but no macOS, Linux or phone app.
  • It is a hosted service. The Windows app is open source; the server is not public and you cannot run your own.
  • It is in beta: an alliance is started with a code from the Warcryer Discord and has room for 300 approved members.
  • You sign in with a Discord account.
  • It is built for organised events. There is no video, screen sharing, file sharing or positional audio, and the text chat exists inside operations only.

Coming over from Discord

  1. Stay on Discord. Warcryer signs in with it and posts your events to a channel you choose.
  2. Start your alliance in the app, create your clans, and send each clan leader an invite link.
  3. Connect a Discord channel on the Alliance tab so operations are announced where your players already read.
  4. Plan the next event as an operation, let players answer Coming or Can't come, build the parties, and go live.

Questions

How many people can be in a Discord voice channel?
Up to 99, according to Discord's developer documentation. Stage channels hold far more, but as an audience with a few speakers. A Warcryer alliance has room for 300 approved members during the beta, in parties that each keep their own talk while all of them hear Broadcast.
Does Warcryer replace Discord?
No. It replaces Discord's voice channels for organised events. Your server, its text channels and its roles stay where they are, and Warcryer announces your events there.
Is there a Warcryer bot for my Discord server?
No bot is needed. Announcements use a webhook that an alliance admin or clan leader pastes into the app; Warcryer never reads your server.
Does it work on phones?
No. Warcryer is a Windows app, with a browser version for a quick look. If your players join events from phones, Discord is the better choice.
