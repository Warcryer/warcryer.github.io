How Warcryer compares

TeamSpeak, Discord, Mumble and Ventrilo give you channels and leave the rest to you: who goes where, who may speak to whom, who signed up, who turned up. Warcryer starts from the event's roster and works those out for you. It also does far less than they do outside an event.

Checked against each product's own pages on 18 November 2026. Warcryer is in beta.

One at a time

Warcryer vs TeamSpeak

TeamSpeak has carried large alliances for twenty years, with channels, whisper lists and a deep permission system that each community wires up for itself. Warcryer starts from the other end: the event's roster decides who hears whom, and the wiring is done for you.

Read the comparison

Warcryer vs Discord

Discord is where most alliances already live, and nothing here asks you to leave it. Its voice channels are made for a room of people talking. A siege with a chain of command is a different shape: hundreds of players, several layers of leaders, and orders that must be heard over party chatter.

Read the comparison

Warcryer vs Mumble

Mumble is free, open source and very good at what it does: low-latency voice on a server you run yourself, with a permission system that can express almost anything. Warcryer gives up that freedom for one thing done for you: the event's roster decides who hears whom.

Read the comparison

Warcryer vs Ventrilo

Ventrilo is the voice many guilds raided on, and it is still here: a current client for Windows, macOS and Linux, and servers hosted by its owner. It keeps to what it always was, a server with channels and a push-to-talk key. Warcryer is built around the event instead of the channel.

What Warcryer does not do

If one of these matters to you, one of the others is the better choice, and the page for it says which.

  • It is a Windows app. There is a browser version for a quick look, but no macOS, Linux or phone app.
  • It is a hosted service. The Windows app is open source; the server is not public and you cannot run your own.
  • It is in beta: an alliance is started with a code from the Warcryer Discord and has room for 300 approved members.
  • You sign in with a Discord account.
  • It is built for organised events. There is no video, screen sharing, file sharing or positional audio, and the text chat exists inside operations only.
