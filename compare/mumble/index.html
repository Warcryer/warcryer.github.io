Warcryer vs Mumble

Mumble is free, open source and very good at what it does: low-latency voice on a server you run yourself, with a permission system that can express almost anything. Warcryer gives up that freedom for one thing done for you: the event's roster decides who hears whom.

Checked against Mumble's own pages on 18 November 2026. Warcryer is in beta.

The short answer

Choose Mumble if

  • You want to run the server yourself and keep every byte under your own control.
  • Open source on both ends matters to you.
  • Your players are on Linux or macOS.
  • You want positional audio, or an overlay drawn in the game.
  • You have someone who enjoys writing ACLs and will keep them right.

Choose Warcryer if

  • Nobody in your alliance wants to run a server or maintain a permission tree.
  • Your events have a chain of command that changes every week with the roster.
  • You want sign-ups, party building and attendance in the same place as the voice.
  • New players should be talking in the right place within a minute of joining, without a setup guide.

What is different in practice

A roster instead of ACLs

Mumble's access control lists and groups can describe a fleet's command structure, and large communities have done it. Somebody has to write it and keep it matching the people. In Warcryer the structure is the roster: make someone a deputy or a party leader and their voice rights change with the role, in that moment.

No shout keys to hand out

In Mumble a leader reaches other channels with whisper and shout shortcuts that each player sets up on their own client. In Warcryer everyone has one key per net they may speak on, and which nets those are is decided for them.

Nothing to host

A Mumble server is yours: to install, secure, update and pay for. Warcryer is hosted, which is less freedom and less work. If self-hosting is the point for you, Mumble wins this one outright.

The event around the voice

Mumble is a voice application and does not try to be anything else. Warcryer also asks who is coming, shows which party lacks a healer, moves players between parties with their audio, and records who attended.

Where Mumble is the better choice

Warcryer does one thing, and it is young. These are its limits today.

  • It is a Windows app. There is a browser version for a quick look, but no macOS, Linux or phone app.
  • It is a hosted service. The Windows app is open source; the server is not public and you cannot run your own.
  • It is in beta: an alliance is started with a code from the Warcryer Discord and has room for 300 approved members.
  • You sign in with a Discord account.
  • It is built for organised events. There is no video, screen sharing, file sharing or positional audio, and the text chat exists inside operations only.

Coming over from Mumble

  1. Keep your Mumble server for everyday talk if you like it. Warcryer is for the events.
  2. Start your alliance in the app and choose the starting lists for your game, or write your own.
  3. Create your clans and send each clan leader an invite link. No certificates or registrations to hand out.
  4. Plan the first operation, build its parties on the board, and go live.

Questions

Is Warcryer open source like Mumble?
Partly. The Windows app is open source under the MIT licence and every release is built from the public repository. The server is not public. Mumble is open on both ends.
Can I self-host Warcryer?
No. It is a hosted service. If you want your own server, Mumble is the better choice.
Does Warcryer have positional audio?
No. Nothing is read from the game, so the app does not know where anyone stands. Voice is organised by the chain of command instead: party, clan, command, broadcast.
Is the latency as low as Mumble's?
We have not measured the two side by side and will not claim it. Warcryer's voice runs over WebRTC with the Opus codec, as most current voice services do.
