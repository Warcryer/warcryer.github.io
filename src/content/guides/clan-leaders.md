# Guide for clan leaders and officers

Read the [members guide](/guides/members/) first. This one covers what your role adds.

A clan leader is appointed by an alliance admin. The leader appoints officers. Officers share the leader's roster powers, with the exceptions noted below.

## Who can do what

| | Leader | Officer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approve or decline join requests | Yes | Yes |
| Remove a member | Yes | Yes, but not another officer |
| Appoint and remove officers | Yes | No |
| Speak on the **Clan** net | Yes | Yes |
| On the **Command** net | Yes | No |
| Build and edit parties of your clan's members in an operation | Yes | Yes |
| Set or change the clan crest | Yes | No |
| Create and revoke invite links | Yes | Yes |
| Create, change and delete constant parties | Yes | Yes |
| Asked to choose a new leader when one of your members drops out as party leader | Yes | Yes |
| Read the clan's log | Yes | No |

The leader can only be replaced by an alliance admin.

## The clan crest

The leader can give the clan a crest. Open **Clan** and click **Upload crest**, then pick a PNG, JPEG or WebP image. The app shrinks it to a small square, so a simple, bold image reads best, and a transparent background suits the dark screens.

The crest then appears beside the clan wherever it shows: the clan's lounge and Clan net, roster boards and the clan lists. Beside your members' names, in voice and on boards with more than one clan, it takes the place of the clan tag. A crest is optional: without one, the tag in brackets is shown as before. Everyone in the alliance can see it. **Change crest** replaces it and **Remove** takes it away; alliance admins can also remove a crest.

## Approving members

A new player picks your clan in the app and waits. You see a number on the **Clan** tab.

1. Open **Clan**.
2. Under **Waiting for approval**, check the in-game name against your clan in game, and the **Discord** name beside it against the player you know.
3. Click **Approve** or **Decline**.

Approve only names you recognise. An in-game name belongs to one account, so a stranger cannot ask under a member's name once that member has signed in; the Discord name tells you who is asking when the member has not. A change of in-game name is written to the log. An approved player hears your clan's lounge, your Clan net and Broadcast during operations, so approval is the gate that keeps strangers out.

The green dot beside a member means they have the app open right now.

## Invite links

Instead of telling every player to find your clan in the list, send them a link. On the **Clan** tab, under **Invite links**, choose how many times the link may be used and for how many days, and click **Create invite link**. **Copy link** puts it on your clipboard.

A player who opens the link sees your clan's name and crest and a button that opens the app. There are two kinds of link:

- **Still needs approval** (the default). The player lands on your waiting list and you approve them as usual. Use this when the link might travel further than you intend.
- **Lets them straight in.** Tick **Let people who use it straight in, without approval** when you create it. There is no approval step: whoever made the link is recorded as having vouched for everyone who uses it. Hand such a link only to people you know, and keep the number of uses small.

A link is a secret. Anyone who gets hold of it can use it until its uses run out, it expires, or you click **Revoke**. Post it in your clan's own private channel, never in a public one. If the leader or officer who made a straight-in link later loses that role, the link only adds people to the waiting list.

## Removing a member

Click **Remove** beside their name and confirm. They are dropped from the lounge, from any party in an operation that has not ended, from the clan's constant parties, and from every net, at once. They can request to join again, and you can decline.

A member or an officer can also leave by themselves, with **Leave clan** on their Clan tab; the same things happen. You, as leader, cannot leave until an alliance admin has appointed another leader.

For a transfer to another clan of the alliance, ask an alliance admin: they move the player across in one step, already approved.

## Officers

The leader clicks **Make officer** beside a member, or **Remove officer** to undo it. An officer can speak on the Clan net from then on, and the app asks them to bind a Clan key the next time they are in an operation.

## Your nets

- **Clan net:** you speak to the whole clan with your Clan key. Members only listen. Use it for clan-level orders, not chatter.
- **Command net (leader only):** you coordinate with the event leader and the other leaders. You count as a senior voice there: while you, the event leader or a deputy speaks, party leaders are made quieter for everyone listening.
- **Lounge:** between operations everyone in the clan can talk freely there.
- **Clan net between operations:** your Clan key works between operations too. Everyone in your clan hears you, in the lounge or in a group, and their own talk is made quieter while you speak. Use it to gather the clan: "World boss up at the ruins, everyone come."
- **Groups:** between operations anyone in the alliance can start or join a small group (see the [members' guide](/guides/members/#groups)). You and your officers can take one of your own members out of a group: click the group on the right of the Voice tab to open its players out, then the **×** beside the name; the alliance's log records it. Members of other clans are their own leaders' to manage.

## Constant parties

A constant party is a group of your members who always fight together: a fixed line-up, in order, with a leader. Set them up once on the **Clan** tab, under **Constant parties**, and they can be put on any operation's board with one click.

- **Create one:** type a name and click **Create**. The clan leader and officers can do this.
- **Add members:** pick them from **Add a member…**. They are kept in the order you add them, and the first one leads it.
- **Change the leader:** **Make leader** beside a member. The leader becomes the party's leader in an operation.
- **Take out** removes a member. **Delete** removes the constant party.
- **Rename:** click its name.

A player is in one constant party at a time: adding them to another moves them. A constant party's own leader can rename it, change its members and hand leadership on, so you can leave each group to look after itself. The count turns red past 9, which is a warning, not a limit.

Every member of the clan can see the clan's constant parties; their own is marked.

## Parties in an operation

When your clan takes part in an operation, open **Operations**, then the operation, to reach the roster board.

You can:

- **Add your constant parties:** each one not yet on the board is offered under **Constant parties**. Click it and it becomes a party, with its members in order and its leader in charge. **Add all** adds every one at once. A member who is already in another party stays there. After that it is an ordinary party: change it as you like, your constant party is not affected.
- **Create a party** with **+ New party**, rename it by clicking its name, and choose its group type (Mage group, Stopper group and so on) under the name.
- **See what each party lacks:** every party shows **Full** or **Not ready**, and **Needs** lists the support roles it has nobody for yet (Buffer, Bard, Recharger, Healer). Click a role to list the unplaced players of that role in Reserve.
- **Place your own members** in your clan's parties: drag a card onto a party or onto Reserve, or click the card and use **Move to…**. Your clan's parties are those led by one of your members, and any that have no leader yet.
- **Choose a party's leader** in your clan's parties: click a member of the party, then **Make party leader**.
- **A party led by another clan** is theirs to arrange. To get one of your members into it, the member clicks **Ask to join** and its leader accepts, or you ask the event leader. You can always take your own member back out to Reserve.
- **Answer join requests** from your own members.
- **Set priority targets:** name the enemies your clan, one of your clan's parties or one of your members is to keep down. Open **Priority targets** on the board, or **Targets** on the operation's panel of the Voice tab during the fight. Choose whom the name is for, type it (several with commas between) and press Enter. Those players see it at the top of their chat and on their strip, and a click on it copies it. Targets for everyone on the operation, and for another clan's parties and players, are for the event leader, deputies and alliance admins to set.
- **Delete a party**, if everyone in it is from your clan.

You cannot move members of another clan, and you cannot start or end the operation. The event leader, deputies and alliance admins can do both.

The first player placed in a party leads it, and party leaders are on Command. If a leader is moved out, the next member takes over, so a party is never cut off from Command. If a leader drops out of a live operation, their party and you (the leader's clan leader and officers) are asked after about 30 seconds to choose a new one; if nobody does, the next member in voice is promoted after 30 seconds more.

Until the operation is started, everyone stays in their lounge while you build the roster. Starting it moves every player into their party at once.

## Your clan's chat channel

During an operation your clan has a channel in the chat ([members' guide](/guides/members/#chat)), read by your members who are in that operation. You and your officers write in it; members read, unless your alliance's admins have opened clans' channels to everyone. It is a good place for what must not be missed: where to regroup, who to assist.

## Discord announcements

Players who do not have the app open do not see that an event has been planned. The clan's leader can have the app say so in a Discord channel: on the **Clan** tab, under **Discord announcements**.

1. In Discord, open the channel's settings (the cog beside its name), then **Integrations**, **Webhooks**, **New Webhook**, and **Copy Webhook URL**. You need the Manage Webhooks permission on that Discord server.
2. In the app, paste the address under **Discord announcements** and click **Connect**. A first message is posted at once, so you can see it arrive.

From then on the channel gets a message when an operation your clan takes part in is created, when its name, start time, leader or clans change, when it starts, and when one that had not started is cancelled. A message names the operation, its start (Discord shows it to each reader in their own time zone), the clans and the event leader. Some time before an operation starts, a reminder is posted too, with how many have said they are coming; your alliance's admins choose how long before, or switch it off.

Nobody is pinged unless you choose a role. Under **Ping**, type `@everyone` or `@here`, or paste a role's ID, and click **Save**: the channel is told which role it is, by name, so you can see you picked the right one. That role is then pinged when an operation is announced, by the reminder, when it starts and if it is cancelled, and not by a change of detail. To copy a role's ID in Discord: switch on **Developer Mode** under Settings → Advanced, then in the server's settings right-click the role and choose **Copy Role ID**. If the ping does not light up for your members, allow the role to be mentioned in its settings. Empty the box and save to ping nobody again.

- **Send a test message** checks the channel still works.
- **Disconnect** stops the messages. Deleting the webhook in Discord does too.
- The address is a secret: anyone who has it can post in that channel. The app stores it and never shows it again, so if you need it elsewhere, copy it from Discord.

Alliance admins can set up one channel for the whole alliance in the same way; a channel used for both gets each message once.

## Attendance

After an operation has ended, open it under **Operations**. For you and your officers the board shows **Attendance**: each of your members, how long they were in voice while the operation ran, when they joined, and whether they had said they were coming. **No show** marks a member who said they were coming and never joined. **Copy for a spreadsheet** puts the table on your clipboard, ready to paste into a sheet. While an operation is running, **Attendance so far** shows the same up to now.

You see your own clan only; the organisers see everyone.

## History: who comes, and how often

The **History** tab reads the attendance that is written down as each operation ends, for your own clan:

- **Turnout by operation:** a column for each operation your clan took part in, as tall as the members you had then, filled as far as those who were in voice. Point at one for its name, date, how long it ran, how many had said they were coming and how many of those did not show. **Show as a table** gives the same figures as rows.
- **Players:** each member with the operations they attended out of those they were expected at, how often they said they were coming and did not show, and the share of the time they stayed. Those who come least are listed first.
- **A player's record:** click a name for every operation, newest first.

Choose how far back to look at the top: 30 days, 3 months, a year, or everything. The records are kept for as long as the operation is, which is until an alliance admin deletes it. You see what a player did as a member of your clan, not what they did in another clan before or after. **My own record** shows yours.

## Your clan's own points

If your alliance's admins allow it, your clan can keep points of its own, for the clan's own loot, beside any the alliance keeps. You and your officers set them up on the **Clan** tab under **The clan's own points**, the same way the alliance's admins set up theirs: start from a preset (Attendance only, Classic DKP, DKP by the hour, EPGP, Zero-sum DKP, Suicide Kings, Fleet participation, or Custom), name it whatever your members say, and change any rule: points for being there, per hour, on time, to the end, coming as said, standby and named objectives, a deduction for not showing, each for every kind of event or only some. Decay, a cap, seasons and **Everyone sees the standings** are there too. You can keep more than one set, up to ten. The alliance admins' guide explains each part.

When an operation your clan took part in ends, its board, and the points tab on **History**, show a draft: each of your members with their time in voice and what the rules suggest, with why. Tick any objectives reached and anyone on standby, change any amount, then **Post**. Nothing is added until you post, and each operation is posted once. Afterwards, give or take points by hand from a member's lines, always with a reason; nothing is ever changed or removed, a mistake is put right with another line. All of it is in the clan log.

If your alliance keeps points too and the standings are not open to everyone, you see your own members' points there.

**Loot.** As a leader or officer of a clan taking part in an operation, you can record its drops on the board, by hand or by reading a screenshot, and give them out in your clan's points (by price, as a loot council, by EPGP, Zero-sum, or a Suicide Kings list). Prices in your clan's points are set on the Clan tab under **Prices in the clan's points**. The alliance admins' guide explains loot in full.

## The clan log

The leader has a **Clan log** at the bottom of the **Clan** tab: who changed what in the clan, newest first. It covers requests, approvals and removals, officers, invite links, constant parties, and what was done to your members in operations (moves, party leadership, bans). Click **Show the log**; use **Filter** to find a name. A line that starts with **The server** was done automatically. Alliance admins can read the whole alliance's log.

## Before an event

- Approve the waiting requests, so nobody is locked out when it starts.
- Check your constant parties are current: new members added, people who left taken out, the right leader. Then one click puts them on the board.
- Set up **Discord announcements** once, so that planning an event tells your clan's Discord by itself.
- Chase the answers. The board shows who is **Coming**, who has not answered and who cannot come. The leader of each constant party can sign the whole party up with one click on the board; you and your officers can do it for them.
- Ask members to set their class and level in **Settings**, so the organiser can see what each party lacks.
- Ask members to open the app early and check the green dots on the board.
- Make sure every party has the leader you want; the leader is marked **» PL**.
- Remind new officers to bind a Clan key, and party leaders a Command key.
